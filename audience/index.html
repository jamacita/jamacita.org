Audience & Access

Measured carefully, reported proportionately.

Jamacita distinguishes human readership from automated access and treats traffic measurement as an integrity question rather than a reason to build unnecessary individual profiles.

The aim is to understand reach, discovery and machine access well enough to make credible statements about the audience without overstating what technical signals can prove.

Human readership

Browser-level usage signals are treated separately from raw network requests so that automated retrieval does not inflate statements about readership.

Automated access

Known search crawlers, AI services and other verified automation are distinguished from probable or unclassified automated traffic wherever the available evidence supports that distinction.

Geographic reach

Country-level traffic describes the approximate location associated with a request or visit. It does not establish nationality, residence or the identity of an individual visitor.

AI-mediated discovery

Human referrals from AI services are conceptually separated from autonomous crawling. Discovery through an assistant and machine retrieval for indexing or training are not treated as the same signal.

Methodology

1. Separate populations
Network requests, human-facing analytics and identified automation are measured as different populations before any aggregate audience statement is made.
2. Evidence before attribution
Technical attribution is limited to what the underlying signal supports. A network or hosting provider is not presented as the identity of the person or organisation behind a request.
3. Aggregate reporting
Long-term reporting favours aggregate measures such as visits, page views, countries, referral classes and automated-access share rather than persistent individual tracking.
4. Versioned definitions
Classification rules and reporting definitions may change as web and AI access patterns evolve. Material methodological changes should therefore be versioned alongside the resulting evidence.

Privacy and interpretation

Audience measurement is designed to remain proportionate to the purpose of understanding reach and access. Jamacita does not treat approximate geography, a network identifier, a browser signal or an automated-access classification as proof of a visitor's personal identity.

Published audience figures, where provided, should therefore be read as measured traffic signals with stated definitions and limits — not as claims about identifiable individuals.

Reporting

Public reporting may include selected aggregate measures once the underlying period is sufficiently meaningful. Methodology is published before headline figures so that reach is not separated from the way it was measured.
